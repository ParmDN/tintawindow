The problem, and a better way to use the space

Privacy where people meet and work

Meetings visible from the corridor

Obscure the areas people can see through while keeping the workplace open to daylight. Coverage should consider both seated and standing views.

Reception glass without a clear finish

Give the arrival area a consistent appearance. Align the treatment with the frames, adjoining panes and the way people move through the space.

Staff areas needing privacy from view

Create a clearer division between public and staff areas. Frosting changes the view through the glass; it does not make a room soundproof.

Offices beside work areas

Screen selected views without creating a solid wall. The finish and light transmission need to suit both sides of the glass.

Clear glass that feels too exposed

A full pane is not always necessary. Selected bands or sections may give the coverage you need while leaving useful clear areas.

Branding that should belong in the room

Patterns or logo-style details can make glass part of your interior. Agree the artwork, scale and alignment before the film is cut and fitted.

Advice, installation & aftercare

Advice for your workplace design

Privacy with a design purpose

Set coverage around people and room use. A band or pattern should obscure the right views without covering useful clear areas.

Artwork agreed before fitting

Review the scale and alignment of any branding beside doors and frames. A whole-partition layout is easier to judge than one isolated panel.

Visual limits explained

Frosting changes what can be seen through glass. It does not soundproof a room, and separate protection needs should be stated early.

How It Works

Agree the design before the film is fitted

01

Identify private and clear areas

Review the partition from both sides, including seated and standing views. Decide which conversations need privacy from view and which routes should remain open to view.

02

Set out bands and patterns

Show doors, handles and neighbouring panes. Agree heights, edges and any artwork so the treatment lines up across the whole space.

03

Approve a suitable finish

Compare the sample’s light, texture and coverage. Where branding is included, confirm the artwork and scale before cutting or fitting is arranged.

04

Plan room access

Confirm the product, layout and person who can sign off the work. Discuss room availability and preparation around staff, furniture and opening hours.

05

Keep the approved layout

Give cleaners the care guidance and retain the design record. It helps with future pane replacement or extending the pattern to another part of the workplace.

Common Questions

Questions about commercial frosted, opaque and decorative film

Does frosted film provide sound privacy?+

No. Frosting obscures views; it does not soundproof a meeting room. If spoken privacy matters, that needs a separate assessment of the room and its construction.

Frosted & decorative options
Can the finish include our logo?+

Suitable decorative treatments can be discussed around your artwork and glass. We confirm the proposed layout and requirements before fitting rather than assuming every design will suit every surface.

Commercial options
Will it block all daylight?+

Frosting admits soft, scattered light. Opaque and blackout products have different light transmission, so check the actual product. The area covered also changes how the room feels.

Commercial options
Can some of the glass stay clear?+

Yes, full and partial coverage can be discussed. Consider seated and standing views, adjoining panes and the appearance from both sides when agreeing the layout.

Commercial options
Can you fit around office hours?+

We discuss access, room availability and the person who can approve the work. Timing and disruption are agreed for the building before installation.
