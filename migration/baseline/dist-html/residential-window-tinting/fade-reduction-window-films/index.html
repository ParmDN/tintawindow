The problem, and a better way to use the space

Where sunlight can fade your interiors

Carpets and flooring fading

Carpets, timber and vinyl flooring can fade unevenly where the sun reaches them each day. Film can help reduce exposure, so your interiors are less directly affected by sunlight.

Furniture fading near the glass

A sofa or chair should be somewhere you enjoy sitting. Reducing UV and managing bright light can help protect its colour, without making the room feel closed off.

Curtains taking the full sun

Curtains and soft furnishings can lose colour with regular exposure. Ask about a film that reduces the sunlight reaching them while keeping a comfortable amount of daylight.

Artwork and finishes worth caring for

Identify the pieces and surfaces you want to protect. Film can be one part of their care, alongside positioning and advice appropriate to the material.

Daylight and a view to enjoy

You chose the windows for the light and outlook. Suitable film can reduce exposure without having to cover the glass whenever the sun comes out.

Heat or glare in the same room

Protecting interiors should also work for the people using the space. We can compare heat and glare performance where the room has more than one problem.

UV protection

Ask for the selected product’s UV performance information. We match the film to your glass; a general percentage cannot tell you what every installation will achieve.

Light and colour

Compare how the film looks on the window and inside the room. You may prefer a lighter finish, or more light control where furnishings receive strong direct sun.

Heat control window films

If the room also gets too hot, discuss solar heat reduction. Reducing heat is a separate performance measure from blocking UV, so check both.

Anti-glare window films

If bright light makes the room hard to use, compare glare control too. The right balance should help you enjoy the room as well as care for its contents.

Common Questions

Questions about fade reduction window film

Can film stop all fading?+

No. UV, visible light, heat and the material itself can affect fading. Film can reduce sunlight-related exposure, but it cannot restore faded colour or guarantee that furnishings will never change.

Fade reduction options
Is a clear film enough?+

That depends on the glass, the product and what is at risk. A clear-looking film may offer UV protection without much light reduction. Where visible light and heat matter too, compare the full product information.

Residential options
Will the room become too dark?+

Different films have different effects on daylight. We help you compare Fade reducing window film in Christchurch using suitable samples, so protection and the feel of the room are considered together.

Residential options
Can you treat just the sunny windows?+

Often that is a useful starting point, subject to suitability. Include adjoining windows in a photograph so we can discuss whether differences in shade or reflection would be noticeable.

Residential options
Can film repair sun-damaged furniture?+

No. Film helps manage future sunlight exposure through the treated glass. Existing damage needs separate care or repair appropriate to the material.
